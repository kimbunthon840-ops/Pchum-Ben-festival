ASTRONOMY & THE KHMER CALENDAR

Why Does Pchum Ben Move Every Year?

Unlike fixed solar holidays, Pchum Ben is governed by the ancient Khmer lunisolar calendar (Chhankitek). The festival always begins on the 1st day of the waning moon of Bhadrapada (ខែភទ្របទ) and concludes on the 15th day (the dark new moon night).

Because a lunar cycle is roughly 29.5 days, a lunar year is approximately 11 days shorter than a solar year. As a result, Pchum Ben fluctuates between late September and mid-October on the Western Gregorian calendar.

🌑

The Dark New Moon

The new moon of Bhadrapada is considered the darkest night of the wet season, creating the ideal mystical veil for ancestral spirits to encounter their living children without the glare of the sun.
