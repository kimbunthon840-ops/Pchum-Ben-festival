SACRED CEREMONIES

Rituals & Customs

Explore the profound ceremonies that have connected Khmer families to their ancestors across generations.

04:00 AM PRE-DAWN RITUAL

Bos Bay Ben (Throwing Rice Balls)

Long before the sun peeks over the horizon, pagoda bells and drums (Skor Thom) resonate into the cool morning air. Devotees dressed in white converge on the temple grounds carrying woven trays of Bay Ben—sticky rice cooked with roasted black sesame seeds and shaped into round balls.

Devotees hold glowing candles and incense, circumambulating the sanctuary (Preah Vihear) three times in a clockwise direction (Padakkhina) in honor of the Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha. At each turn, they toss rice balls onto the temple earth or into designated stone platforms, calling out their departed ancestors' names.

MERIT DEDICATION

Bangskol: Monk Blessings for Ancestral Liberation

Bangskol is the most solemn and meritorious ceremony of Pchum Ben. Families bring urns containing the bone ashes of their ancestors, or place a consecrated white cloth (Pangskulika) before seated Buddhist monks.

As monks hold the ceremonial cords and chant ancient Pali verses reflecting on impermanence (‘Anicca vata sankhara...’), devotees pour purified water (Chhoet Teuk) drop by drop into silver vessels. This symbolic act establishes a conduit channeling the living's generosity directly into the spiritual realm.

SANGHA DANA

Tak Bat (Morning Alms) & White Silk Attire

At mid-morning, long tables of alms bowls are lined up. Devotees dress in traditional white blouses (Ao Pak) and Khmer silk Sampot, representing spiritual purity, humility, and respectful mourning.

Each person places spoonfuls of freshly steamed rice, Num Ansom cakes, curries, and tropical fruit into the monks' bowls, creating an ocean of collective generosity.

DAY 15 FAREWELL

Sron Pralung: Releasing Ancestor Rafts

On the evening of Pchum Thom (Day 15), as the 15-day spiritual gates close, families craft small ceremonial boats from banana trunks and leaves. They load them with Num Ansom slices, puffed rice, betel leaves, candles, and incense.

The glowing boats are gently set adrift on rivers, ponds, and canals, safely guiding ancestral spirits back to their spiritual realms with blessings for their living children.
