CROWN JEWEL OF PCHUM BEN

Num Ansom (Chrouk & Chek)

Num Ansom is Cambodia's quintessential ceremonial cake. Sticky rice seasoned with coconut milk and salt is wrapped around a rich filling: either marinated pork belly with yellow mung beans and crushed black pepper (Num Ansom Chrouk), or sweet ripe baby bananas (Num Ansom Chek).

Tightly rolled inside fresh green banana leaves and tied with bamboo twine, the cakes are boiled in massive drums over firewood for 8 to 12 hours. Culturally, the cylinder shape symbolizes the Shiva Lingam, representing creation and masculine ancestral strength.

🌿 Banana Leaf Wrap ⏳ 10-12 Hr Slow Boil 🔱 Shiva Lingam Symbol
MASTERCLASS

How to Wrap Num Ansom Step-by-Step

👉 Click each step to learn

Step 1: Preparing & Softening Banana Leaves

Fresh wild banana leaves (ស្លឹកចេកណាំវ៉ា) are harvested and gently passed over hot charcoal embers or dipped into boiling water. This softens the natural fibers so they don't tear when rolled tightly into cylindrical packages.

💡 Pro-Tip: Wipe both sides clean with a dry cotton cloth to preserve aromatic green tea notes.

Step 2: Soaking & Seasoning Sticky Rice and Yellow Mung Beans

High-grade Khmer glutinous rice (អង្ករដំណើប) is soaked for 4 hours, rinsed thoroughly, and tossed with thick coconut milk and sea salt. Split yellow mung beans are soaked separately and steamed until tender.

Step 3: Layering the Pork Belly Core (or Banana)

Spread a bed of seasoned sticky rice over double-layered banana leaves. Add a channel of yellow mung beans, then lay a strip of pork belly marinated in Kampot black pepper, garlic, and fish sauce right down the center. Cover with more rice.

Step 4: Folding & Bamboo String Binding (ចងចំណង)

Roll the leaf tightly into a firm cylinder and fold both ends neatly into envelope triangles. Tie securely using soaked bamboo ribbons (ខ្សែបន្ទះឫស្សី) every 2 inches to withstand the hours of violent boiling without bursting.

Step 5: The 10–12 Hour Communal Firewood Boil

Place leftover banana stems at the bottom of a large drum to prevent scorching. Stack the wrapped cakes tightly, cover with fresh water, and simmer over steady wood fire through the night. Family members take turns tending the embers.

VILLAGE SPECIALTY

Kralan (Bamboo Roasted Sticky Rice)

Famous in Battambang and Kratie, Kralan is made by stuffing sticky fragrant rice, black beans, grated coconut, and thick coconut milk inside special young bamboo stalks (Phthek bamboo).

The bamboo tubes are plugged with coconut husk and slowly roasted over glowing charcoal mounds. The inner bamboo membrane fuses with the sticky rice, imparting an unforgettable smoky, sweet, and nutty aroma.

🎋 Natural Bamboo 🥥 Rich Coconut Milk 🔥 Charcoal Roasted
PYRAMID SWEETS

Num Korm & Bay Ben

Num Korm is a delicate pyramid-shaped glutinous rice cake folded in banana leaves. Inside lies sweet caramelized coconut with roasted sesame seeds, or savory yellow bean mash. Its triangular form symbolizes the Yoni (representing Uma / Parvati), complementing the masculine Num Ansom.

Bay Ben are the small rounded sticky rice balls infused with black sesame seeds that devotees mold with their hands at 3 AM to scatter during Bos Bay Ben circumambulation.

COSMIC DUALITY

The Sacred Balance: Num Ansom & Num Korm

Why these two cakes are always prepared as a pair across Khmer cultural ceremonies.

🥖

Num Ansom (នំអន្សម)

  • Shape: Cylindrical (មូលទ្រវែង)
  • Symbolism: Shiva Lingam (លិង្គព្រះឥសូរ) — Masculine cosmic energy, vitality, family strength.
  • Primary Fillings: Marinated pork belly & mung bean, or sweet banana.
  • Cooking Method: 10-12 hour continuous boiling in water drum.
🔺

Num Korm (នំគម)

  • Shape: Triangular pyramid (រាងត្រីកោណ)
  • Symbolism: Yoni (យោនីព្រះនាងឧមា) — Feminine grace, fertility, maternal warmth.
  • Primary Fillings: Caramelized shredded coconut with palm sugar & toasted sesame.
  • Cooking Method: Steamed in tiered bamboo steamer baskets.
