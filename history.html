ANCIENT CHRONICLES

Origins & Legends

From Buddhist canonical sutras of King Bimbisara to the royal decrees of the Khmer Empire and King Ang Duong.

CANONICAL BUDDHIST ORIGIN

The Legend of King Bimbisara

In the Buddhist scriptures (Khuddaka Nikaya, Petavatthu), King Bimbisara of Magadha heard terrifying shrieks in his palace at night. Troubled, he approached the Buddha Gotama, who explained that former relatives from ninety-two aeons ago had fallen into the Preta realm due to stealing monastic food. The spirits were starving and weeping because no one had offered merits on their behalf.

The Buddha instructed the King to present lush offerings of food, robes, and clean water to the Sangha, and explicitly dedicate the merit (Pattidana) to these spirits. Upon doing so, celestial ponds, golden clothing, and nectar instantly manifested for the relieved ancestors.

9TH - 13TH CENTURY ANGKOR

Angkorian Stele Inscriptions & Royal Ancestor Temples

Ancestor veneration has been the bedrock of Khmer civilization long before modern times. Angkorian kings dedicated their grandest state temples to their ancestors: King Indravarman I built Preah Ko for his parents; Jayavarman VII dedicated Ta Prohm to his mother and Preah Khan to his father.

Epigraphic inscriptions in Sanskrit and Old Khmer detail seasonal distribution of rice, sesame seeds, ghee, and cloths for ancestral rites, proving an unbroken link to today's Pchum Ben.

19TH CENTURY REIGN OF KING ANG DUONG

The 15-Day Kann Ben Codification

In the mid-19th century, King Ang Duong (reigned 1848–1860) standardized and unified Khmer religious celebrations at the Royal Court in Oudong. Observing that monks were trapped indoors during the muddy monsoon rains (Vassa) and that villagers struggled to sustain them all at once, he decreed the 15-day system: 14 days of rotating shifts (Kann Ben 1 to 14) followed by the grand unified celebration on Day 15 (Pchum Thom).

This brilliant civic and spiritual organization ensured that every resident participated, every pagoda remained fully provisioned, and no wandering soul went hungry.
